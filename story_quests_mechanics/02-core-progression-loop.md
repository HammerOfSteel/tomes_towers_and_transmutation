# 02 — Core Progression Loop

## Purpose

This document defines the **actual game loop** that should govern content, reward design, quest structure, and future implementation planning.

The goal is to prevent the project from becoming a collection of adjacent systems.

Instead, every system should plug into one shared macro loop:

1. **Discover knowledge**
2. **Take risks in the field**
3. **Bring resources and truths back to the tower**
4. **Transform them into new magical capacity**
5. **Strengthen allies and defenses**
6. **Face stronger narrative and systemic pressure**
7. **Push outward again**

---

## The Three Nested Loops

The game should be designed around three nested loops that feed one another.

## Loop 1 — Moment-to-Moment Combat Loop
This is the second-to-second action layer.

### Player actions
- move
- dodge
- cast
- strike
- spare / recruit
- gather pickups
- manage cooldowns and spacing

### Desired feel
- readable
- responsive
- escalating
- expressive through spells and companions

### Outputs from this loop
- survival
- immediate loot
- encounter completion
- chance to recruit enemies
- access to chests, books, relics, or doors

This loop is what makes the game feel like an action RPG.

---

## Loop 2 — Expedition Loop
This is the 5–20 minute adventure loop.

An expedition may be:
- a tower floor push
- a procedural dungeon run
- a biome-specific gathering route
- a quest-driven ruin visit
- a hunt for a specific tome or reagent
- a defense-response mission

### Expedition input questions
Before leaving the tower, the player should be deciding:

- What am I trying to obtain?
- Which floor / dungeon / biome is most likely to contain it?
- Which spells and potions suit that risk?
- Which companions should come with me?
- Do I need combat strength, gathering efficiency, or recruit capture utility?

### Expedition outputs
An expedition should potentially return:
- tomes
- grimoires
- annotations
- monster captures or recruits
- reagents
- crafting materials
- relic tools
- quest evidence
- settlement/faction standing changes
- defensive intelligence
- rare conversion catalysts

This loop is what makes the game feel like an RPG journey rather than a combat arena.

---

## Loop 3 — Domain Loop
This is the long-term growth layer and the game’s defining differentiator.

Back at the tower, the player should be able to:
- reclaim new tower functions
- read and catalogue books
- unlock research branches
- transmute raw loot into refined materials
- brew potions
- mutate spells
- assign companions
- prepare wards, traps, and response doctrines
- resolve story consequences
- brace for tower assaults or regional backlash

### Domain loop outputs
The tower loop should create:
- new power options
- stronger strategic identity
- visible growth of the princess’s rule
- stronger future expeditions
- new story states
- stronger defense readiness

This loop is what makes the game feel like *Tomes, Towers & Transmutation* specifically.

---

## Canonical Macro Loop

This should become the project’s main design reference:

```text
[Discover a lead]
    ↓
[Choose an expedition target]
    ↓
[Delve / fight / spare / gather / investigate]
    ↓
[Return to the tower]
    ↓
[Read, research, transmute, brew, assign, prepare]
    ↓
[Resolve quest and story consequences]
    ↓
[Trigger or anticipate external pressure]
    ↓
[Defend, stabilize, or retaliate]
    ↓
[Unlock deeper targets]
    ↓
[Repeat at a higher magical tier]
```

Every major reward in the game should feed one of five progression vectors.

## 1. Knowledge Progression
This includes:
- tomes
- grimoires
- footnotes
- ritual diagrams
- species-specific magical archives
- monster research entries
- defensive doctrines

### What it unlocks
- spells
- spell schools
- spell modifiers
- research options
- tower floor functions
- new quest branches
- true understanding of Solmor’s plans

---

## 2. Material Progression
This includes:
- reagents
- herbs
- essences
- monster parts
- relic fragments
- arcane residues
- minerals
- processed alchemical outputs

### What it unlocks
- potions
- catalysts
- rituals
- spell mutation recipes
- defensive supplies
- advanced crafting
- floor restoration requirements

---

## 3. Social / Companion Progression
This includes:
- spared enemies
- tamed beasts
- followers from quests
- familiars
- settlement allies

- elite recruits

### What it unlocks
- combat support
- field utility
- defense assignments

## 4. Spatial / Domain Progression
This includes:
- reclaimed floors

- stabilized territories

- more systems
- more production
- more research capacity
- more defense options
- more story staging
- more world influence

This includes:
- species arc advancement
- general quest states

## How the Loops Support the Title

## Tomes in the loop
Tomes enter at:
- expedition discoveries
- quest rewards
- hidden archives
- rare dungeon branches
- settlement scholars
- tower library restoration

Tomes affect:
- combat
- research
- transmutation
- story truth
- access to deeper content

## Towers in the loop
Towers matter because:
- everything returns there
- rooms become systems
- companions need assignment space
- research happens there
- assault events test preparedness
- reclaimed floors are visible progression
- the player’s identity is anchored there

## Transmutation in the loop
Transmutation links the return phase to the next expedition:
- refine loot into useful forms
- convert weak reagents into potent catalysts
- create potions for riskier runs
- mutate spells for new enemy problems
- prepare defense consumables
- solve quest problems through crafted magical means

## The Reward Ecology Rule

A reward should never be “just a reward.”

Each reward must answer:
1. What loop does this belong to?
2. What future decision does it create?
3. How does it make the player differently powerful, not merely numerically stronger?

### Examples

#### Good reward
A grimoire that unlocks storm magic research and reveals that automata are weak to conductance overload.
- It gives new build options
- It changes combat planning
- It adds a research path
- It ties knowledge to future dungeon choices

#### Weak reward
“+50 gold” with no relation to knowledge, transmutation, companions, or tower growth.
- Numerically useful
- Thematically thin
- Does not deepen the game’s identity

Gold/currency can still exist, but it should not be the main emotional reward.

## Expedition Archetypes

To keep the loop varied, expeditions should come in several meaningful types.

## 1. Tome Hunt
Goal:
- secure a specific book or grimoire

Primary rewards:
- knowledge progression
- puzzle / key progression
- school mastery

Typical costs:
- dangerous elite rooms
- locked archives
- rival factions
- puzzle or defense gates

---

## 2. Reagent Run
Goal:
- gather ingredients or magical substances needed for craft, research, or ritual work

Primary rewards:
- material progression
- potion access
- ritual preparation

Typical costs:
- environmental hazards
- biome specialization
- limited carry capacity or rare-node routing

---

## 3. Recruitment Expedition
Goal:
- capture or persuade a useful companion, follower, or monster unit

Primary rewards:
- social progression
- defense depth
- field utility

Typical costs:
- suboptimal combat choices
- non-lethal handling
- escort or morale risks

---

## 4. Story Expedition
Goal:
- resolve a species or general quest beat

Primary rewards:
- narrative progression
- knowledge or social unlocks
- domain consequences

Typical costs:
- branching consequences
- faction changes
- longer-term narrative lock-ins

---

## 5. Suppression / Response Expedition
Goal:
- address a threat before it becomes a larger tower or territory problem

Primary rewards:
- reduced danger
- defensive intelligence
- resource security
- faction stability

Typical costs:
- time pressure
- stronger enemy compositions
- opportunity cost versus proactive exploration

---

## Pressure and Escalation

The world should not stay passive while the player grows.

As the princess becomes more powerful, several pressure systems should escalate:

- factions become alarmed
- monsters are displaced or attracted
- Solmor’s research footprint becomes more visible
- unstable magic causes anomalies
- rare tomes generate attention
- the tower itself becomes a strategic target

### Why this matters
Without pushback, the tower becomes a static reward sink.
With escalating pressure, the tower becomes the center of a living campaign.

---

## Recommended Cadence of Tower Defense Events

Defense should be **punctuating**, not constant.

### Desired cadence
A defense event should occur:
- after a major power breakthrough
- after certain story reveals
- after claiming strategically visible territory
- after prolonged neglect of threat buildup
- at authored narrative milestones

### Avoid
- constant random attacks
- frequent interruptions that prevent exploration rhythm
- defense events that feel disconnected from prior player actions

### Desired feeling
The player should think:

**“Of course they’re coming now. I knew this would provoke something.”**

Not:

**“Why is there another random tower defense mission?”**

## Build Expression Across the Loop

A coherent build in this game should not be only a combat loadout.

A build should include:

- chosen spell schools
- spell mutations
- potion doctrines
- preferred companion composition
- favored expedition type
- chosen defensive doctrine
- story / moral style where relevant

### Example builds

#### Archive Pyromancer
- seeks grimoires and library ruins
- uses burn, catalysts, and volatile potion chains
- prefers fragile ranged companions
- prepares incendiary defense supplies

#### Slime Transmuter
- specializes in reagent conversion and adaptation
- values absorption, mimicry, and stable-form crafting
- recruits durable frontline followers
- resolves several quests through crafted solutions instead of brute force

#### Royal Beast-Marshal
- focuses on commanding recruited units
- chooses aura buffs, formation tools, and morale boosts
- uses dungeons to secure elite companions
- excels in tower defense events more than pure solo burst damage

This is the intended level of identity richness.

## Failure and Recovery in the Loop

Failure should redirect play, not hard-stop it.

## If the player fails an expedition
Possible consequences:
- lost consumables
- injury/debuff
- missed opportunity window
- threat meter increase
- certain reagents spoiled or lost
- captured recruit escapes

But the player should still return with:
- knowledge of what is there
- map discovery
- narrative signals
- refined plan for next attempt

## If the player fails a defense event
Possible consequences:
- room damage
- temporary lockout of a tower station
- wounded / lost followers
- stolen resources
- faction emboldenment
- research setback

But not:
- catastrophic campaign reset
- total domain invalidation
- punishment so severe the player avoids the system entirely

Failure should create **new goals**, not dead ends.

---

## Implementation Implications

This loop design implies several concrete production rules:

1. **Tower return must feel meaningful every time.**
   The player should almost always have at least one useful tower action after an expedition.

2. **Expeditions need targeted intent.**
   The world should offer reasons to choose one destination over another.

3. **Defense requires preview and preparation.**
   The game must surface threats before they fully land.

4. **Books and materials require categorization.**
   They must support planning, not inventory clutter.

5. **Companions require role clarity.**
   Followers need understandable use cases in field and defense play.

6. **Quest rewards should plug into loop vectors.**
   Rewards must create new decisions, not just completion signals.

---

## Final Rule

If a new feature does not strengthen at least one of these loop transitions, it should not be prioritized:

- field → tower
- knowledge → new capability
- loot → transformation
- companion → strategy
- story → new systemic pressure
- defense → renewed outward push

That transition logic is the heart of the game.
