# 03 — Tomes and Grimoire Progression

## Purpose

This document formalizes the **Tomes** pillar.

The project already establishes that spells are learned through books rather than abstract menus. This document expands that idea into a full progression structure so that books become:

- a core exploration reward
- a build-defining system
- a story-delivery method
- a research gate
- a dungeon and quest motivation layer

The player should not merely unlock “spells from books.”

The player should build a magical identity by assembling a **personal library of dangerous knowledge**.

---

## Core Principle

Not all books are equal.

The game should treat magical texts as a hierarchy of knowledge objects with different mechanical roles.

## Knowledge Object Hierarchy

### 1. Notes
Short texts, marginalia, scraps, diagrams, field journals, Solmor comments.

**Function:**
- micro-lore
- passive bonuses
- weak hints
- monster/dungeon clues
- recipe fragments
- defense warnings

### 2. Tomes
Standard magical books that introduce or deepen a spell, school, or tower function.

**Function:**
- unlock spells
- unlock school access
- enable research topics
- reveal transmutation recipes
- open floor/quest interactions

### 3. Grimoires
Rare, high-risk, build-defining texts.

**Function:**
- unlock advanced doctrine
- mutate existing spells
- introduce dangerous tradeoffs
- alter defense options
- influence story/faction reactions

### 4. Archives
Collections rather than single books: Solmor’s records, candidate journals, species archives, dungeon libraries.

**Function:**
- reveal story truth
- unlock quest branches
- enable broad research categories
- grant large thematic progression rather than one spell

---

## Book Types by Gameplay Role

Each book should fit at least one of these roles.

## A. Spell Acquisition Books
These teach a new active or passive spell.

Examples:
- introductory elemental texts
- self-defense manuals
- ritual primers
- specialized combat grimoires

### Reward feeling
Immediate build expansion.

---

## B. School Deepening Books
These do not necessarily teach a brand-new spell, but deepen a school the player already knows.

Examples:
- combustion theory
- conductive pathways
- nature-spirit pact theory
- void boundary notes

### Reward feeling
The player’s current build becomes more expressive or more efficient.

---

## C. Research Unlock Books
These unlock new projects in the tower.

Examples:
- a text on volatile reagent stabilization
- bestiary notes on automaton cores
- a surgical guide to preserving ectoplasm
- a handbook on ward lattice repair

### Reward feeling
The player gains long-term tower and transmutation possibilities.

---

## D. Narrative Revelation Books
These contain evidence, history, or concealed truth.

Examples:
- candidate journals
- sealed notes from Solmor
- prior princess records
- old faction correspondence
- species-specific “you were here before” documents

### Reward feeling
The world and the player’s role become more legible and more disturbing.

---

## E. Defensive Doctrine Books
These teach how to prepare the tower against specific threats.

Examples:
- anti-siege warding guides
- beast-herding manuals
- emergency alchemical response procedures
- summoning anchor diagrams

### Reward feeling
The player becomes strategically smarter, not just stronger.

---

## F. Access / Key Books
These reveal passwords, symbolic procedures, hidden room logic, or ritual permissions.

Examples:
- observatory cipher volumes
- laboratory seal procedures
- buried archive maps
- species-specific resonance texts

### Reward feeling
Knowledge itself functions as a key.

---

## Book Rarity Tiers

Books should have rarity not only for loot excitement, but to clearly communicate importance.

## Common — Primer
- teaches foundational ideas
- usually one immediate unlock
- often found in early floors, classrooms, studies, libraries

## Uncommon — Manual
- teaches a specialized application
- often introduces combo logic or deeper research use
- found in side rooms, ruins, or guarded collections

## Rare — Tome
- powerful unlock or strong research bridge
- usually tied to elite encounters, puzzle rooms, quest beats, or hidden archives

## Legendary — Grimoire
- build-defining, risky, often narratively charged
- tied to boss spaces, species arc peaks, late tower rooms, or uniquely dangerous dungeons

## Forbidden — Solmor / Candidate Archive Class
- reveals ascension truth, prior candidate interventions, unstable doctrine
- can affect multiple systems at once
- should feel narratively and mechanically volatile

## School Structure Recommendation

The current magic design names multiple elements and spell categories. To make tomes feel coherent, the player should think in **schools** rather than isolated abilities.

## Recommended major schools

### Arcane
Theme:
- force, wards, precision, lattice logic, magical architecture

Supports:
- magic bolt variants
- wards
- barriers
- structural tower defense prep
- spell-slot or cooldown refinement

### Pyromancy
Theme:
- combustion, volatility, pressure, overcharge

Supports:
- flame darts
- burns
- chain reactions
- explosive catalysts
- risky transmutation outcomes

### Stormcraft
Theme:
- lightning, conductivity, chaining, overload

Supports:
- chain lightning
- stun arcs
- automaton disruption
- conductive traps
- anti-swarm defense

### Umbromancy / Shadow
Theme:
- concealment, draining, fear, mobility, thresholds

Supports:
- stealth
- phase effects
- execution-style abilities
- panic/flee interactions
- recruit capture support

### Vital / Nature Alchemy
Theme:
- growth, reagents, toxins, restoration, beast resonance

Supports:
- potion and herb systems
- poisons
- greenhouse synergy
- creature taming support
- healing / sustain

### Gravity / Transmutative Matter
Theme:
- pull, reshape, alter, convert, destabilize

Supports:
- gravity well
- matter conversion
- spell mutation
- ritual crafting
- high-tier transmutation and defense mechanics

## How Books Interact with Spells

A spell should not usually be unlocked once and then forgotten.

Each spell should have **four possible knowledge states**.

## State 1 — Seen
The player has encountered references to the spell or school.
- appears in notes
- referenced by NPCs or quest texts
- visible in book catalog silhouette form

This creates anticipation.

## State 2 — Learned
The player acquires the primary book and can cast the spell.

This creates immediate new agency.

## State 3 — Studied
The player finds follow-up texts or annotations that deepen the spell.
Possible outcomes:
- reduced cooldown
- altered status effect
- added synergy tag
- unlocks transmutation recipe connected to the spell

This creates mastery.

## State 4 — Transmuted
The player has researched the spell deeply enough to alter its nature.

Possible outcomes:
- element shift
- area pattern change
- utility/combat tradeoff
- companion interaction version
- defense-prep version

This creates build identity.

---

## Example Spell Knowledge Ladder

### Flame Dart
**Seen**
- references in burned notes and pyromancy marginalia

**Learned**
- basic Flame Dart spell

**Studied**
- burn stacks can persist longer
- unlocks ember catalyst recipe
- reveals certain enemies spread flame status

**Transmuted**
choose one route:
- **Searing Dart** — higher direct damage, weaker burn
- **Cinder Thread** — weaker hit, spreads burn to nearby targets
- **Beacon Dart** — marks enemies, tower defenders focus them during defense events

This is the model all core spells should follow.

## Tome Placement Rules

Books should not be placed as random generic drops only.

Placement must communicate meaning.

## Placement categories

### 1. Guaranteed Core Placement
Used for foundational progression books.
- first defensive text
- first projectile text
- first research primer
- first school-introducing book per major stage

Purpose:
prevent dead seeds and ensure coherent progression.

### 2. Weighted Dungeon Placement
Used for most tomes and manuals.
- library ruins
- occult vaults
- alchemy dungeons
- elite archive rooms
- observatories
- collapsed study chambers

Purpose:
support replay and discovery.

### 3. Quest Placement
Used for species arcs, Solmor truths, faction correspondence, specialized doctrines.

Purpose:
tie authored story to mechanical unlocks.

### 4. Defense / Event Placement
Some books should arrive only after surviving or resolving assaults.

Purpose:
make the world pressure system feed knowledge progression too.

## Dungeon Types and Their Knowledge Bias

To make exploration choices meaningful, dungeon families should have knowledge profiles.

## Tower interior floors
Best for:
- Solmor notes
- core arcane texts
- ascension records
- floor-function manuals
- candidate archives

## Library ruins
Best for:
- school deepening books
- faction histories
- grimoires with incomplete context

## Alchemy vaults
Best for:
- transmutation doctrines
- potion treatises
- reagent stabilization theory
- dangerous process manuals

## Tombs / barrows
Best for:
- shadow, undeath, memory, spirit pact books
- personal journals
- moral choice texts

## Beast lairs / nature shrines
Best for:
- taming guides
- beast resonance theory
- herbology and fungal alchemy
- nature-affinity texts

## Observatory / star ruins
Best for:
- celestial, gravity, void, territory, omen texts
- late-stage story revelations

This makes destination choice legible.

## The Personal Library System

The player should gradually assemble a visible and mechanically meaningful library in the tower.

## Functions of the personal library
- catalog discovered books
- show school coverage and gaps
- reveal which spells are merely learned vs studied vs transmuted
- surface unread or unanalyzed texts
- connect books to research unlocks
- provide story archive browsing

### Why this matters
Without a library layer, books are just inventory items or one-time triggers.
With it, the tower visibly becomes a growing seat of power.

## Recommended library views
- by school
- by rarity
- by system use (spell / research / story / defense)
- by origin (tower, dungeon, settlement, quest, faction)
- by unresolved implications (“this text mentions a place you have not found”)

## Species and Book Affinity

The same book system should express species identity.

Each species should have:
- favored schools
- unique reactions to certain book families
- at least one species-only archive chain
- at least one species-specific “truth” book series

### Example directions

#### Human
- command, duty, discipline, field doctrine
- books around rulership, military procedure, and legitimacy

#### Undead
- memory, death, continuity, remains, soul preservation
- books that question whether undeath is defect, curse, or tool

#### Vulperia
- cunning, contracts, hunt doctrine, social leverage
- books related to tracking, deceit, and intelligence networks

#### Slime
- cohesion, mimicry, dissolution, adaptation
- books that blur biology, alchemy, and selfhood

#### Elf
- recursion, memory, age, repetition, old authorship
- books that imply former cycles and long arcs of magical history

#### Celestial
- law, signal, altitude, impact, broken communications
- books tied to ward theory, sky structures, cosmic governance

#### Draconic
- territory, inheritance, combustion, sealed claims
- books tied to ancient ownership, elemental right, and weaponized pride

## Story Truth Through Books

Books are one of the best ways to preserve the tone of Solmor’s design:
methodical, eerie, and uncomfortably reasonable.

The player should repeatedly discover that books are:
- mechanically useful
- emotionally invasive
- historically revealing

### Key story book categories

#### Candidate Journals
Document prior successors.
Use for:
- emotional foreshadowing
- unique school variants
- future questline hooks

#### Solmor Work Logs
Reveal his logic, not just his guilt.
Use for:
- research unlocks
- tower function restoration
- moral discomfort

#### Prior-Princess Records
Reveal repeating patterns or species-specific truths.
Use for:
- arc-specific revelations
- identity destabilization
- late-game choices

#### Faction Records
Explain the world’s perspective on towers, wizards, and magical authority.
Use for:
- settlement quest branches
- defense pressure justification
- external political stakes

## Defensive Knowledge

Some books should specifically change how the player handles assaults.

Examples:
- field manuals on beast lanes
- notes on ward anchor spacing
- anti-construct response primers
- evacuation and containment protocols
- lure-and-cull bestiaries

### Mechanical outcomes
A defense doctrine book might unlock:
- new assignment options for followers
- better warning previews for certain threat types
- room-specific defense bonuses
- consumable recipes specialized for one assault family
- tower AI/auto-response improvements in late game

This helps the “towers” pillar remain knowledge-driven instead of RTS-driven.

---

## Open-Ended Spellcrafting Guardrail

Books must enable build variety without making spellcrafting unreadable.

### Rule
Books should unlock **structured mutation options**, not infinite arbitrary editing.

For example:
- branching mutations
- doctrine sockets
- catalyst tags
- school synergies
- defense conversion variants

Not:
- full freeform parameter editing of every number or property

### Why
The fantasy should be:
**“I learned enough forbidden theory to transform this spell.”**
Not:
**“I opened an unbounded engineering sandbox.”**

---

## Implementation Implications

This design implies the following production needs:

1. **Book taxonomy**
   The game needs typed knowledge objects, not only generic lore entries.

2. **Catalog state tracking**
   The player must track seen / learned / studied / transmuted states.

3. **Placement logic**
   Worldgen and quest content need book families and location weighting.

4. **School metadata**
   Spells must know which school and mutation ladder they belong to.

5. **Research bridge**
   Books must unlock more than immediate casts; they must feed tower systems.

6. **Visible library return**
   The player should feel the tower gaining intelligence as well as power.

---

## Final Rule

A book is successful content only if it does at least one of the following:

- changes how the player fights
- changes what the player can research
- changes what the player understands
- changes where the player wants to go next
- changes how the player prepares the tower

If it does none of those, it is flavor text and should be treated as secondary content, not a pillar mechanic.
